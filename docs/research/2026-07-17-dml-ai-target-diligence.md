# dml.ai acquisition target diligence

**Research date:** 2026-07-17  
**Target:** `dml.ai`  
**Scope:** public-source screen of the asset, seller, operating activity, domain status, and transaction risks. This is not a formal valuation, legal opinion, or trademark clearance.

## Bottom line

`dml.ai` is an **asset-only domain purchase**, not an acquisition of an operating company. On 2026-07-17 the site was a generic Spaceship “domain for sale” page. Its page source exposed an offer-only listing with `minOfferPrice: 1000`, formatted as **US$1,000**. The page says an offer must be higher than the seller's minimum. There is no advertised buy-now price. [Live sale page](https://dml.ai/)

No product, proprietary software, team, customer base, revenue, profit, or other business assets are disclosed. No seller legal entity can be tied to the domain from public records: the registry record names a privacy service rather than the beneficial registrant. Therefore an earnings or balance-sheet valuation is not possible. The purchase price should be justified entirely by the domain's brand utility, scarcity, possible clean history, and the buyer's alternatives.

The strongest observable negotiation fact is the public US$1,000 minimum. A minimum-level opening offer is rational unless external comparable-sale work supports a higher number. Paying a premium should be conditional on proof of clean title/control and clean history; the seller has not publicly substantiated traffic or revenue.

## Market valuation and offer range

The useful comparison class is **three-letter, alphabetic `.ai` domains**, not headline one-word sales such as `bot.ai` or current marketplace asking prices. Asking prices are seller aspirations; completed sales are the better evidence.

The [NameBuzz short-domain dataset](https://namebuzz.co/domains/short), which aggregates reported sales from sources including NameBio, DNJournal, marketplaces, and public reports, contained 730 three-letter alphabetic `.ai` sales when checked on 2026-07-17. Calculated from that dataset:

- median sale: **US$1,840**;
- middle 50%: approximately **US$1,275–US$3,063**;
- mean sale: approximately **US$11,771**, showing how a small number of exceptional names distort the average;
- 655 of 730 reported sales were **US$10,000 or less**.

**Indicated market range: US$2,000–US$5,000.** The lower half is the defensible acquisition range. The upper end assumes a buyer who has committed to using DML as an active AI-facing brand.

Spaceship says SellerHub commission is normally 10%, and the parties may negotiate who pays it or split it. Confirm the allocation before accepting a counteroffer. [Spaceship SellerHub fees](https://www.spaceship.com/sellerhub/)

For a purely defensive holding, redirect, or speculative purchase, do not use the exceptional ceiling: cap the all-in spend near **US$2,000**. For an active company, email, and API identity, the normal US$4,000 all-in cap is reasonable. Any higher amount should be supported by a written five-year business case comparing `dml.ai` with the best available alternative.

## Red flags and conditions before paying

1. **Seller identity is hidden.** Require the marketplace to confirm that the seller controls the registrar account and has authority to transfer the domain.
2. **Transfer lock is present.** `client transfer prohibited` is commonly a registrar lock, but completion must be conditional on a successful internal push or unlock/auth-code transfer into an account controlled by the buyer.
3. **No operating assets are evidenced.** The offer must not assume software, content, customers, traffic, email lists, social handles, or trademarks.
4. **History is not established.** Review historical pages, backlink quality, search-index status, malware/phishing blocklists, and email/domain reputation before using the name. Any seller traffic claim should be verified with read-only analytics access.
5. **Trademark risk is not cleared.** “DML” is a short acronym with many possible users. Run UK and intended-market trademark searches for the planned goods/services before purchase. The registry expressly subjects `.ai` names to UDRP.
6. **The seller appears experienced and patient.** The portfolio evidence suggests a domain investor with many short `.ai` names. Do not reveal the buyer's maximum or strategic urgency in the first offer.
7. **Price is not supported by financial performance.** The public US$1,000 minimum is a seller-set floor, not evidence of market value.

## Spaceship legitimacy and customer experience

**Conclusion:** Spaceship is a legitimate registrar and a reasonable channel for this purchase, but SellerHub is a relatively new marketplace and should not be treated as a fully guaranteed escrow service. The likely outcome is a routine transfer when the seller's control and listing are current; the meaningful residual risk is cancellation/refund after payment when an externally registered name has a stale or disputed listing.

- **Legitimacy.** ICANN's current accredited-registrar list includes **Spaceship, Inc.** (IANA ID **3862**). The official `.ai` RDAP record for `dml.ai` also identifies it as the current registrar. This verifies the registrar, not the identity or reliability of the beneficial seller. [ICANN accredited registrar list](https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/accredited-registrars/list-of-accredited-registrars) and [official `dml.ai` RDAP record](https://rdap.identitydigital.services/rdap/domain/dml.ai).
- **What SellerHub protects.** SellerHub's agreement makes an accepted offer binding and requires its intermediary process. It says a buyer receives a 100% refund if the name cannot be transferred for technical or administrative reasons. However, Spaceship describes itself as a marketplace *venue*, says it is not a party to the buyer-seller agreement, and disclaims liability for buyer-seller disputes. The specific refund promise is in its lease-to-own section, so obtain written confirmation that the same remedy applies to this cash purchase. [Domain Marketplace Agreement](https://www.spaceship.com/legal/domain-marketplace/).
- **Independent broad customer signal.** Trustpilot showed **4.4/5 from 4,141 reviews** on 2026-07-17 (77% five-star; 10% one-star; 705 reviews in the preceding 12 months). Praise concentrates on prices, UI, and ordinary domain/hosting use; complaints include support and account/domain problems. These reviews are predominantly registrar/hosting experiences, not evidence of SellerHub completion. Trustpilot itself says it does not fact-check individual claims, and the profile is merged and paid. [Trustpilot profile](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/spaceship.com).
- **Marketplace-specific evidence is thin and mixed.** SellerHub launched publicly in 2025, so it has a much shorter track record than established aftermarket platforms. Community reports include smooth sales, but also buyer reports of a paid purchase being cancelled/refunded after a seller said they no longer owned the externally registered domain. Those anecdotes do **not** establish fraud, but they show the practical stale-listing/seller-performance risk. A separate buyer reported that delivery by free push at the incumbent registrar versus a paid auth-code transfer felt unusual; domain-community replies describe this as normal transfer mechanics. [SellerHub launch context](https://www.spaceship.com/blog/2025-year-results/), [cancellation report](https://www.reddit.com/r/Domains/comments/1tbjo7o/spaceship_sold_me_a_domain_then_cancelled_because/), and [transfer-mechanics discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Domains/comments/1tfg8g9/spaceship_buying_process_seems_odd/).

For `dml.ai`, before a binding offer ask SellerHub support in writing: (1) whether seller ownership/control has been verified; (2) whether an accepted cash offer can be cancelled and, if so, refund timing; (3) whether delivery will be an internal push or auth-code transfer; and (4) the exact buyer-side fee. Pay by card, keep all confirmations, and do not make off-platform payment.

## Transaction checklist

- Submit the first offer through the current Spaceship page at the minimum accepted amount; if the form interprets “higher than” strictly, use **US$1,001** rather than volunteering a larger jump.
- Keep a pre-agreed walk-away ceiling based on comparable three-letter `.ai` sales and the cost/adequacy of alternative names.
- Use the marketplace's controlled payment and transfer process; do not pay directly in response to an off-platform contact.
- Before release of funds, require proof of control, confirmation that no dispute or security interest exists, and explicit written scope stating that `dml.ai` is the asset transferred.
- On completion, move the name into a buyer-controlled registrar account, enable strong unique credentials and MFA, replace all contacts/nameservers, enable transfer lock, and configure DNSSEC where supported.
- Preserve the invoice, transfer confirmation, seller representations, and the pre-transfer RDAP record.
