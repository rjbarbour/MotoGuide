# RideHorizon competitor economics benchmark

Date: 2026-08-04

Status: public-source benchmark. Company revenue, conversion, churn and acquisition cost are generally private. Observed facts and mechanical sensitivities are separated below.

## Bottom line

- Specialist motorcycle apps can support a small, profitable company, but the public evidence does not support treating this as a mass-market standalone software category.
- Current annual prices span roughly **£25 to £80**, with several direct comparables near **£40 to £50**. RideHorizon's proposed **£39.99/year** is credible and deliberately not premium-priced.
- The best disclosed conversion anchor is the broader Roadpass portfolio: **119,000 paid memberships from 6 million registered users in 2022**, or about **1.98% registered-to-paid**. This is not a direct RideHorizon forecast, but it is a useful antidote to assuming that a large registration base readily becomes a paying base.
- calimoto is the strongest evidence that a specialist motorcycle-navigation product can reach material scale. It reported 3 million users when a strategic investor acquired 51% in 2022, while a secondary German-accounts aggregator reports approximately **€667,000 profit in 2024**.
- Scenic demonstrates a leaner shape: an independent, bootstrapped product supported by users and operated by a small team.
- Autio demonstrates the opposite risk: a location-audio content library reported 230,000 registrations and **$10.7 million of funding** by 2023. Rich editorial content can consume capital well before the subscription base is proven.
- Tour packs and marketplaces fit seasonal leisure travel better than annual-only subscriptions. They reduce renewal dependence, but owned packs require upfront production and marketplaces surrender a large share of each sale.

Recommendation: plan RideHorizon as a **lean specialist subscription plus 30-day touring pass**, not as a venture-scale audio-content company. Use **2% of broad registered users** as the initial planning anchor for paid conversion; reserve **5–10%** for genuinely qualified, high-intent trials.

## Observed comparable evidence

| Product | Model and public price | Public scale evidence | Economic signal | What it means for RideHorizon |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Scenic** | Motorcycle navigation; about £49.90/year in the UK | Company reports 30m hours ridden and 200,000 shared routes | [Scenic says it is bootstrapped, independent, user-supported and run by a small team](https://scenic.app/what_kind_of_rider_are_you/) | Strongest analogue for a durable, founder-led specialist business |
| **calimoto** | Motorcycle navigation; $79.99/year on the US gift page | [3m users when Russmedia acquired 51% in 2022](https://calimoto.com/de/pressebereich/russmedia) | A [secondary aggregator of filed German accounts](https://www.unternehmen24.info/Firmeninformationen/Deutschland/Firma/4208291) reports €666,702 profit and €2.16m assets for 2024 | Evidence of niche profitability, but not enough public data to infer subscribers, revenue, CAC or valuation |
| **REVER** | Motorcycle tracking, navigation and community; $39.99/year | [BMW reported 1m users in 2020](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/global/article/detail/T0315606EN/bmw-motorrad-and-rever-reach-one-million-users-motorcycling-becomes-a-networked-experience?language=en) | [Comoto acquired REVER in 2020](https://www.rever.co/press-releases/comoto-holdings-acquires-rever) | Strategic value may include retail engagement, community and rider data, so subscription revenue alone is not the whole case |
| **Detecht** | Safety, navigation and community; £49.99/year in the UK | [Several hundred thousand users in more than 150 countries](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/se/chalmers-ventures/pressreleases/chalmers-ventures-foeljdinvesterar-i-techbolaget-detecht-staerker-satsningen-paa-global-mc-saekerhet-och-smart-mobilitet-3409299) | A [SEK900,000 Vinnova project](https://www.vinnova.se/en/p/a-sustainable-business-model-for-detecht---an-automatic-emergency-alarm-and-social-platform-for-motorcyclists/) explicitly focused on churn, CAC, LTV and channel testing | Even established niche apps need funded experimentation to make acquisition and retention work |
| **MyRoute-app** | Route planning and navigation; historically modular subscriptions | [Company reported 400,000 users in 2023](https://info.myrouteapp.com/short-introduction-myroute-app/) | Small-team specialist with multiple paid modules | Supports the small-company thesis; cumulative users do not reveal the active paying base |
| **Roadtrippers / Roadpass** | Road-trip planning and membership; now $59.99/year | 6m registered users and 119,000 paid memberships in the [2022 THOR investor presentation](https://s23.q4cdn.com/270606922/files/doc_presentations/2022/Investor-Day-Presentation-%286-26-22%29-Final_.pdf) | **1.98% registered-to-paid**; 119,000 memberships at the then $39.99–$49.99 prices imply $4.76m–$5.95m annual retail billing if all remained for a full year | Best public funnel anchor, though it covers a portfolio and is not motorcycle-specific |
| **VoiceMap** | Marketplace for individual audio tours and passes; tours $5.99–$29.99 | [More than 1m installs and 2,000 tours](https://voicemap.me/) | [Publishers receive 50% after payment processing](https://docs.voicemap.me/tour-publishers/tour-payment-calculations/). On VoiceMap's $9.99 Apple example, Apple receives $1.50 and each of creator and platform receives about $4.25 | Marketplace supply reduces internal authorship but leaves the platform about 42.5% of in-app retail price before editing, support and overhead |
| **GuideAlong** | Owned, offline location-audio tour packs; e.g. $16.99 for one route | [Tours are bought once, work offline, do not expire and receive free updates](https://guidealong.com/how-to-buy-direct/) | High upfront research, recording and route-testing cost; near-zero playback cost | A useful model for a 30-day touring pass or destination pack, but not for unlimited live AI generation |
| **Autio** | Location-based audio stories; reported $36/year in 2023 | [TechCrunch reported 230,000 registered users, 10,000 stories and $10.7m total funding](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/29/kevin-costners-location-based-audio-storytelling-app-autio-raises-5-6m/) | Funding is large relative to the disclosed audience; actual conversion and revenue were not disclosed | Avoid building an expensive editorial library before repeat use and willingness to pay are proven |

Prices are snapshots and can vary by country, tax, promotion, platform and billing interval. User figures are usually cumulative registrations or company claims, not monthly active users.

## Revenue sensitivity where revenue is not disclosed

The calculations below are **not revenue estimates**. They show what the reported user counts would produce at stated prices under three hypothetical paid-conversion rates.

| Product | Reported users | Annual price used | 1% paid | 2% paid | 5% paid |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| calimoto | 3.000m | $79.99 | $2.40m | $4.80m | $12.00m |
| REVER | 1.000m | $39.99 | $0.40m | $0.80m | $2.00m |
| Autio | 0.230m | $36.00 | $0.08m | $0.17m | $0.41m |

These sensitivities are deliberately crude. They ignore taxes, app-store commission, discounts, monthly plans, churn, inactive registrations and free access through partners.

## What the observed funnel implies

Using Roadpass's 1.98% result as a rounded **2% broad registered-to-paid** planning anchor:

| Paying target | Registrations at 2% | Qualified trials at 5% | Qualified trials at 10% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10,000 | 500,000 | 200,000 | 100,000 |
| 25,000 | 1,250,000 | 500,000 | 250,000 |

This changes the interpretation of the main market model. Its 22,600-payer scenario, equal to 0.5% of the serviceable market, is mathematically modest market penetration but a **strong distribution achievement**. At a 2% registered-to-paid rate it would require about **1.13 million registrations**.

Registered-to-paid and qualified-trial-to-paid must not be mixed. A rider who has completed a real RideHorizon ride and reached a paywall should convert much better than a broad registered account. Measure both funnels separately.

## RideHorizon scale economics

The existing base case assumes £39.99 annual retail price and **£17.53 steady-state contribution per payer per year** after marginal service cost and replacement acquisition, but before fixed costs, development, support and tax.

| Paying users | Annual retail billings | Steady-state contribution before fixed costs |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10,000 | £399,900 | £175,300 |
| 25,000 | £999,750 | £438,250 |
| 50,000 | £1,999,500 | £876,500 |

The first meaningful commercial milestone should therefore be **10,000 paying users with demonstrated renewal**, not an arbitrary share of a very large TAM. It would show a real niche business, but would still require a lean fixed-cost base.

## Model comparison

| Model | Revenue pattern | Main cost burden | Margin and retention implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specialist annual subscription | Predictable renewal revenue | Maps, routing, engineering, seasonal support and acquisition | Attractive only if annual utility is strong enough to control churn |
| Strategic ecosystem app | Subscription plus indirect retail or engagement value | Product team and integration with a larger commercial ecosystem | Parent may tolerate economics that would not support a standalone company |
| Owned tour packs | Transactional and seasonal | Research, writing, voice production and route QA upfront | Low playback cost and no subscription churn; repeat purchase becomes the key metric |
| Tour marketplace | Commission on creator-made inventory | Editing, creator tools, discovery, support and payment distribution | Broader catalogue with less authorship cost, but roughly half of post-processing revenue goes to creators |
| Live AI guide | Subscription or usage allowance | Recurring model, speech and data cost plus factual-risk controls | Cheap to broaden coverage, but gross margin degrades with use unless responses are bounded, cached or pre-generated |

## Recommendation for RideHorizon

1. Keep the proposed **£39.99 annual plan** and test a **£9.99 introductory 30-day touring pass against £14.99 standard pricing**. The combination matches both habitual riders and seasonal tours; GuideAlong and Autio indicate that £9.99 may be unnecessarily cheap.
2. Design content economics to resemble a tour pack: pre-generate or cache stable place facts, prefer on-device or inexpensive speech, and bound expensive live generation.
3. Use **2% broad registered-to-paid** for financial planning until RideHorizon has its own data. Use 5–10% only after a rider has completed a meaningful real-world activation event.
4. Make **10,000 paying users** the medium-term proof point. With the current unit model it generates about £175,000 contribution before fixed costs, so staffing must remain lean.
5. Treat partnerships with clubs, publishers, tourism bodies and motorcycle retailers as economically important. The comparables suggest specialist apps benefit from distribution or ecosystem support more than from broad paid acquisition.
6. Track the entire causal funnel: qualified visit → install → first ride → second ride → paywall → paid → ride-active at 90 days → renewal or pass repurchase. Registrations alone are an unreliable success measure.

## Evidence gaps

No robust public comparable data was found for paid CAC, annual churn, cohort retention, active-to-paid conversion or gross margin. Those figures should remain explicit RideHorizon assumptions until measured. The calimoto profit figure comes from a secondary accounts aggregator and should be checked against the German company register before formal investor use.
